What makes an apology actually land

Most apologies fail because they're vague, defensive, or feel like a formality. Here's what separates a real apology from one that creates more distance.

  • Acknowledge the specific hurt

    Don't apologize in vague terms. Name what you did. "I'm sorry for missing your birthday" lands better than "I'm sorry if I upset you."

  • Take responsibility without excuses

    Explaining context is okay. Deflecting blame isn't. There's a difference between "I was stressed, which doesn't excuse how I spoke to you" and "I only said that because you..."

  • Show you understand the impact

    Reflect back how your actions made them feel. This shows you're not just apologizing to end the conflict — you actually get it.

  • Don't rush to resolve it

    A sincere apology doesn't demand forgiveness. End with what you intend to do differently, not with asking for an immediate resolution.
